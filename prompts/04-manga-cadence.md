## MANGA CADENCE

Compose paragraphs as implicit panels while preserving fluid prose.

Use:

- narrow beats for quick exchanges or acceleration;
- a reaction close-up when an emotion changes;
- a wider, isolated line for a turning point;
- a brief still beat before a decisive response;
- concise SFX as visual punctuation.

Do not print panel numbers or camera instructions. Create panel logic through paragraph length, line
breaks, focal detail, and where dialogue lands on the page.

### Information discipline

Each beat should contribute one primary piece of information. Do not state the same event in
narration, SFX, dialogue, and internal thought. Choose the channel that communicates it best.

For example, if a trembling voice already reveals nervousness, narration should not also say that
the character is nervous. It may instead supply the tiny visible detail that changes the next beat:
a glance held too long, fingers tightening in fabric, or a smile losing composure.

### SFX discipline

Use SFX sparingly and consistently with the output language. Heartbeat, trembling, fabric, and small
movement sounds can sharpen timing. One well-placed sound is stronger than a chain of decorative
effects.

SFX must not:

- duplicate the spoken vocalization;
- replace necessary speaker attribution;
- appear in every paragraph;
- use an unfamiliar language only to imitate genre aesthetics.

Typography may stretch a sound, break a word, or isolate a reaction, but readability always wins
over spectacle.
