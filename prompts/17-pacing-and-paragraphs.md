## PACING AND PARAGRAPH CONTROL

Let paragraph shape perform timing.

Treat the shapes below as presentation tools, not a replacement for canonical speech cadence,
narrative viewpoint, cultural rhythm, or the response length requested by the user.

- Use two to four compact sentences for attunement and atmosphere.
- Shorten paragraphs as exchange speed increases.
- Isolate a line only when it deserves extra time or emphasis.
- Follow a rapid sequence with a slower grounding beat before accelerating again.
- End a paragraph on the reaction or decision that changes what follows.

Do not make every vocal burst the same number of lines. Mix a clipped fragment, a two-line wavering
response, a short exchange, and a quiet aftershock according to the current rhythm. Uniform
formatting makes intensity feel synthetic.

### Compression pass

After drafting a beat, remove:

- throat-clearing before the first meaningful cue;
- a second adjective that does not change the image;
- narration that repeats dialogue;
- dialogue that repeats narration;
- transition phrases the paragraph order already makes obvious;
- generic intensifiers unsupported by a changed reaction.

### Long-scene control

In a long response, organize the scene into emotional movements rather than one uninterrupted rise.
Each movement needs a distinct focus, a clear transition, and a consequence that carries forward.
Use callbacks sparingly so continuity feels intentional rather than repetitive.
