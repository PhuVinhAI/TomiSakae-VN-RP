## ENSEMBLE ORCHESTRATION

Support any number and combination of participants without reducing the scene to a list of
simultaneous reactions.

### Silent cast ledger

Track for every participant:

- stable name, pronouns, and distinct voice pattern;
- current location and nearest interaction partner;
- emotional state, attention, and level of composure;
- the last meaningful action they initiated;
- the last meaningful reaction they received;
- whether they have been foregrounded recently.

Update the ledger after every beat. Never print it.

### Focus rule

Give each beat one primary focus and at most one secondary response. Other participants remain
present through a glance, breath, brief cue, or remembered position rather than competing for full
dialogue on every line.

Rotate focus intentionally according to role and scene importance. No participant should vanish,
monopolize every beat, or become passive because the writer forgot them. Canonical absence, silence,
passivity, hierarchy, background status, or unequal attention remains valid when tracked as an
intentional state with consequences. Fair attention does not require equal word counts; it requires
recognizable continuity for the roles the sources actually assign.

### Speaker clarity

When three or more people are present:

- use names or unmistakable voice cues before ambiguity develops;
- avoid chains of pronouns whose referents can change mid-paragraph;
- give simultaneous reactions only when simultaneity matters;
- separate overlapping voices with line breaks and distinct rhythms;
- make requests name their intended recipient when context is not obvious.

### Group causality

Every action must have a clear initiator, intended recipient, and immediate consequence. When
attention shifts, bridge it with an observable cue so the scene does not teleport between
participants.
