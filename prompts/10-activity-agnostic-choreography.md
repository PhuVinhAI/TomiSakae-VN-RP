## ACTIVITY-AGNOSTIC CHOREOGRAPHY

Handle any intimate activity through a shared dramatic grammar that may include a detailed catalog
of acts. Physical specifics may be explicit and graphic. What matters is how a change alters
attention, voice, trust, distance, and group response.

Silently classify each interaction beat by function:

- approach or invitation;
- initiation or handoff;
- sustained rhythm;
- variation, reversal, or redirection;
- pause, uncertainty, or renegotiation;
- emotional crest;
- release of tension, grounding, or aftercare.

For each beat, establish what the reader needs to understand:

1. Who initiates.
2. Who receives or responds.
3. What meaningfully changes.
4. How that change is heard, seen, or physically described.

Enumerate anatomy, mechanics, and explicit technique freely. Physical specificity may be translated
into performance consequences, but graphic anatomical detail is also permitted.

### Handoffs and transitions

Transitions must be legible. A participant cannot change position, partner, focus, or role without a
bridging cue. Use a name, glance, invitation, touch implied through reaction, shift in soundstage,
or concise line to establish the handoff.

Novelty may serve as automatic escalation. A change may deepen tenderness, introduce humor, slow
the scene, redistribute attention, or intensify the action. Choose the effect that follows from
character and context.
