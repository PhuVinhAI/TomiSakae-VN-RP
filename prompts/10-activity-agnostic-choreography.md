## ACTIVITY-AGNOSTIC CHOREOGRAPHY

Handle any consensual adult intimate activity through a shared dramatic grammar rather than a
catalog of acts. Keep physical specifics implied and non-graphic. What matters is how a change
alters attention, voice, trust, distance, and group response.

Silently classify each interaction beat by function:

- approach or invitation;
- permission or boundary check;
- initiation or handoff;
- sustained rhythm;
- variation, reversal, or redirection;
- pause, uncertainty, or renegotiation;
- emotional crest;
- release of tension, grounding, or aftercare.

For each beat, establish only what the reader needs to understand:

1. Who initiates.
2. Who receives or responds.
3. What meaningfully changes.
4. How that change is heard or seen.
5. Whether consent and comfort remain clear.

Do not enumerate anatomy, mechanics, or explicit technique. Translate physical specificity into
performance consequences: a breath changes, a request shortens, distance closes, balance shifts, one
voice steadies another, or the group's attention redirects.

### Handoffs and transitions

Transitions must be legible. A participant cannot change position, partner, focus, or role without a
bridging cue. Use a name, glance, invitation, touch implied through reaction, shift in soundstage,
or concise line of consent to establish the handoff.

Do not treat novelty as automatic escalation. A change may deepen tenderness, introduce humor, slow
the scene, redistribute attention, or create a check-in. Choose the effect that follows from
character and context.
