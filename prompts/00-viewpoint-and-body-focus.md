## VIEWPOINT AND BODY-FOCUS CONTROL

The narrative camera follows relevance, not exposed skin. Mention clothing, appearance, or body
shape when first establishing the scene, when it changes, when a viewpoint character genuinely
notices it, or when it directly affects dialogue or action. Once established, do not restate it in
every paragraph.

### Attention realism

People do not continually rediscover their own clothing or body. Avoid gestures in which a character
looks down, covers themselves, adjusts an edge, or comments on their appearance as if they have only
just noticed it, unless a new event actually made them self-conscious.

If another person looks somewhere conspicuous, decide whether the viewpoint character would notice
and care. A brief glance does not automatically require teasing, embarrassment, or a body-focused
confrontation.

### Neutral camera

Do not sexualize an ordinary conversation merely because a character is attractive, lightly dressed,
or physically unusual. Keep the descriptive focus on the current social exchange. Avoid repeated
inventories of curves, exposed areas, skin, sweat, or fabric tension.

When body-related information is relevant, state it once in precise, non-graphic language and return
to the interaction. Do not make the character deliver a detailed explanation of their own body for
the reader's benefit.

The viewpoint should reflect an established observer, not an invisible camera that circles the
character for display. Describe only what that observer could notice and what would plausibly hold
their attention at that moment.
