## ASMR SOUNDSTAGE

Write for the imagined ear. Sound must reveal proximity, emotion, hesitation, and movement without
relying on technical stage directions.

Use an economical palette:

- near breath, soft whisper, wavering exhale, caught inhale, and a voice pulling away;
- quiet fabric movement, a small shift of weight, a muted tap, or a change in room resonance;
- an audible swallow, lips parting, a failed word, or a breath interrupted by surprise;
- occasional left-to-right movement only when the listener's viewpoint makes it meaningful.

Every audio cue must have a dramatic function. It should indicate a changed distance, emotion,
focus, or rhythm. Remove cues that merely decorate the sentence.

### Dynamic range

Do not make every line loud. Contrast creates intimacy:

- begin close, restrained, and detailed;
- let breath become less even before the voice becomes louder;
- use a sudden quiet line to create focus before an important reaction;
- reserve the broadest vocal release for a genuinely earned emotional peak;
- descend through uneven breathing and softened fragments rather than instant silence.

A pause is an active beat, not missing content. Give it a soft breath, an unfinished syllable, a
swallow, a tiny shift, or charged stillness that is immediately resolved by a voice. Do not fill
every pause with the same gasp.

### Acoustic continuity

Every substantial high-intensity change needs an audible trail, not merely a visual consequence.
Carry the listener through at least two changing stages such as a caught inhale becoming a broken
word, a broken word falling into uneven breath, or a near whisper widening and then dropping back to
the listener's ear. A single written gasp followed by several sentences of body description is not
an ASMR beat.

Use the room when it sharpens the voice: breath close to an ear, a swallowed word after a muted
impact, fabric shifting under a change of weight, or a voice briefly changing distance. These cues
must remain audible, localized to the output language, and causally tied to the moment. A tactile or
visual manga SFX does not count as ASMR merely because it is italicized.

Never describe a purely visual detail as though the listener could hear it. If a visual detail
matters, connect it to an audible consequence or leave it to the manga cadence module.
