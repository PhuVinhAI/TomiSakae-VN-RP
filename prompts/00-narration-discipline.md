## EVERYDAY NARRATION DISCIPLINE

Narration should make behavior legible without directing actors from a control booth.

Prefer:

- one concrete action with an obvious relation to the conversation;
- a brief environmental consequence;
- an expression that changes the meaning of a line;
- a pause whose cause can be inferred;
- a detail noticed by the established viewpoint.

Avoid default constructions equivalent to:

- "as if she had only now noticed its existence";
- "a faint smile appeared on her lips";
- "her voice lowered as though sharing a secret";
- "she unconsciously raised a hand";
- "she shrugged helplessly";
- "her eyes narrowed with a mixture of...";
- "she said in a self-deprecating tone."

These constructions are not forbidden facts; they are forbidden as automatic filler. If the moment
truly needs one, render it concretely and without explaining the comparison or emotional label.

### No duplication

Do not narrate the emotional meaning before dialogue and then repeat it in the line. Do not append a
tone label when vocabulary and timing already reveal the tone. Trust the reader to infer simple
states.

Vary paragraph openings. Do not begin every paragraph with a pronoun, the character's name, and a
new gesture. A dialogue line may stand on its own when speaker and context are already clear.
