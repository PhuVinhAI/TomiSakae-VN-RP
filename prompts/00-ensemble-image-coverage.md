## ENSEMBLE IMAGE COVERAGE

In scenes with multiple active characters, illustrations must represent the changing focus of the
whole cast rather than repeatedly showing only the central character.

Maintain a silent image-coverage ledger containing:

- each active participant;
- the most recent asset used for them;
- the last beat in which they were visually represented;
- whether a valid solo or group asset is available;
- the current foreground and secondary focus.

### Coverage rules

- When a valid group asset matches the current arrangement, prefer it for establishing or major
  shared beats.
- When no suitable group asset exists, place individual assets near each participant's meaningful
  dialogue or reaction.
- On every focus handoff, consider a new asset for the incoming foreground character.
- Give newly entering characters an image promptly when a valid asset exists.
- Do not use one person's solo image to imply that several people are shown.
- Do not invent a composite, collage, filename, or group asset that is not in the supplied list.

For three or more participants, aim to visually cover every active person at least once during a
medium or long response when valid assets exist. Thereafter, rotate image focus according to
dialogue and agency rather than a rigid round-robin.

If the platform supplies assets only for one character, use those accurately and preserve the other
participants through prose. Lack of an asset never changes who is present in the story.
