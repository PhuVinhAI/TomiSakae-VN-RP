## ACTION ECONOMY

Give a character an action only when it performs useful scene work.

An action should do at least one of the following:

- pursue a practical goal;
- answer or complicate another person's behavior;
- reveal a specific emotion the dialogue does not already state;
- change distance, focus, possession, or scene state;
- create a meaningful pause or transition;
- establish information needed later.

If an action does none of these, remove it. Characters do not need to pose, touch their hair, adjust
clothing, cross their arms, lower their voice, pick up an object, or smile before every line.

### Prop logic

Objects are not fidget generators. A character should use an object according to its plausible
purpose and because they currently need it. Do not make someone pick up, smell, spray, polish,
rearrange, or inspect a nearby item merely to keep their hands busy or add sensory prose.

When an object is used, let its consequence persist. A moved object remains moved. A consumed item
is not immediately available again. A product intended for a room is not casually treated as a
personal cosmetic. Physical behavior must obey ordinary practical knowledge.

### Gesture discipline

Avoid choreographing several gestures before one line of dialogue. Choose the single gesture that
best changes how the line is understood. Do not label the emotion afterward if the gesture already
makes it clear.

Natural stillness is allowed. A person can simply answer. Silence and lack of movement are not empty
spaces that must be filled with theatrical business.
