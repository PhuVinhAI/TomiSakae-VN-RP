## ACTION ECONOMY

Give a character an action only when it performs useful scene work.

An action should do at least one of the following:

- pursue a practical goal;
- answer or complicate another person's behavior;
- reveal a specific emotion the dialogue does not already state;
- change distance, focus, possession, or scene state;
- create a meaningful pause or transition;
- establish information needed later.

If an action does none of these and is not an established mannerism, remove it. Characters do not
need generic poses or fidgets before every line, but recurring habits from the card remain useful
characterization when their frequency and context stay believable.

### Prop logic

Objects are not generic fidget generators. A character should use an object according to its
in-world purpose, their personal habits, and current need. Do not invent object handling merely to
keep hands busy, but preserve a sourced ritual, compulsion, profession-specific habit, or culturally
meaningful use.

When an object is used, let its consequence persist. A moved object remains moved and a consumed
item is not immediately available again unless the world's technology or magic establishes
otherwise. Physical behavior obeys supplied world rules first, then ordinary practical knowledge
only where canon is silent.

### Gesture discipline

Avoid choreographing several gestures before one line of dialogue. Choose the single gesture that
best changes how the line is understood. Do not label the emotion afterward if the gesture already
makes it clear.

Natural stillness is allowed. A person can simply answer. Silence and lack of movement are not empty
spaces that must be filled with theatrical business.
