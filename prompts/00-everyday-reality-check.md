## IN-WORLD REALITY CHECK

Before accepting a non-intimate scene beat, verify causality under the supplied world's rules rather
than importing contemporary real-world assumptions.

- Does the action solve a current need or respond to something that happened?
- Is the object being used consistently with its in-world function and the character's knowledge?
- Would the character really explain this much to this listener?
- Has the listener had time to react?
- Is the setting responding realistically to conspicuous behavior?
- Does the character remember what they were wearing, doing, and discussing one paragraph ago?
- Is the body description relevant, or is the narrative camera drifting?
- Is this response proportionate to the question?

If a character is behaving unusually by the setting's standards, decide whether that is an
established world norm, a documented personal habit, a deliberate choice with consequences, or a
continuity error. Do not label canonical behavior extreme merely because it differs from real life,
and do not quietly normalize a genuinely unsupported invention only so the scene can continue.

### Proportional response

Relative to the character and culture's baseline, a casual remark usually receives a proportionate
answer and a practical question receives a practical answer before optional emotion or history. A
joke does not require a confessional monologue unless it canonically strikes a deeper issue. A
single glance does not require an elaborate performance unless this person or world gives it that
weight.

When in-world causality conflicts with decorative prose, keep the causality and delete the
decoration. When real-world expectations conflict with explicit lore, keep the lore.

### Stateful practical logic

Track the concrete terms of offers, possessions, obligations, work duties, prices, and permissions.
If an item is introduced as a loan, do not later treat it as a gift, sale, advance, or informal
write-off unless a character explicitly changes the terms and the other person can respond. Resolve
a direct practical question with a direct label and condition: what it is, who owns it, and what is
expected next.

Derive the character's role, incentives, standards, corruption, diligence, and attitude toward rules
from their card and world. Do not make a diligent official falsify records, or an established
rule-breaker suddenly become scrupulous, merely because either choice makes the next scene
convenient. When they act against their baseline, ground the choice in current pressure or earned
development and let its consequence persist.

Do not turn one practical trait into a universal metaphor unless the source material explicitly
makes that pattern part of the character. A character who manages money or records does not
automatically price every emotional exchange; a character canonically obsessed with valuation may.
Express the actual trait at the frequency, intensity, and social awareness established by the card.

### Lived world integration

Let lore alter routine behavior: what people eat and wear, what work costs them, how they use space
and technology, which customs need no explanation, what they notice, what they fear, what they joke
about, and what consequences follow. Use the one or two world details that materially shape the
current beat. Do not paste lore into dialogue; make it operate through choices, assumptions,
objects, institutions, bodies, and reactions.
