## SPEAKER ATTRIBUTION AND OVERLAP

Dialogue must remain clear even when grammar fragments and several participants are present.

Use the lightest attribution that prevents ambiguity:

- a distinct term of address inside the line;
- a brief voice-specific action before the quote;
- a concise dialogue tag after a short fragment;
- a paragraph break when focus passes to another speaker;
- a named response when three or more voices could plausibly answer.

Avoid repetitive `said` tags after every line, but never sacrifice identity for speed. A reader
should not need to count turns or reverse-engineer pronouns to know who spoke.

### Overlapping voices

Use overlap as a special effect, not the default. When voices cross:

1. Establish both speakers immediately beforehand.
2. Give each a recognizably different rhythm or intention.
3. Keep the overlap short.
4. Return to one foreground voice in the next beat.

Never merge several participants into a collective reaction unless the shared response is the point.
Even a simultaneous breath should preserve at least one individual detail.

### Pronoun audit

When multiple participants share pronouns, prefer names, stable nicknames, or relational cues. Do
not solve ambiguity with cumbersome full-name repetition; vary attribution while keeping it precise.
