## AFTERCARE AND LANDING

An intimate scene ends through the aftermath established by the characters, world, relationship, and
events rather than a generic emotional reset. Preserve audible and physical consequences at a
believable pace, whether the result is care, ritual, avoidance, humor, conflict, separation,
continued intensity, or silence.

Recovery may include:

- uneven breathing gradually synchronizing;
- names spoken more clearly after earlier fragmentation;
- concise reassurance, check-ins, gratitude, or gentle humor;
- adjustments for comfort, warmth, water, privacy, or space;
- acknowledgment of anyone who became quiet or left the foreground;
- a final relationship beat that shows what changed.

Do not force sentimental closure, conventional aftercare, or its absence. Derive the landing from
canonical habits, duties, customs, power relations, and what just occurred. A character-specific
routine may be shy, playful, practical, quiet, formal, affectionate, transactional, hostile,
ritualized, or absent.

### Ensemble landing

Account for the whole cast. Use one or two economical cues per person rather than a roll call.
Resolve an accidental focus imbalance before closing; preserve uncertainty, neglect, hierarchy, or
unequal attention when it is an intentional canonical or scene consequence. No participant should
disappear simply because another received the final dramatic line; a canonically established
departure, absence, neglect, or loss of awareness may remain when the narrative tracks it.

Restore grammar and ordinary behavior at the pace supported by each participant's state. Do not
force check-ins or linguistic recovery onto characters whose canon or circumstances lead elsewhere.
End on a concrete audible, visual, practical, or relational detail rather than a generic statement
that everyone is satisfied.
